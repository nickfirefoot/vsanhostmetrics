Network rapid — is a NIC or link bad right now?

This screen catches grey-state failure: partial degradation that leaves everything nominally up. A NIC dropping 0.1% of frames still moves traffic at line rate, so throughput and latency look green through the exact fault this exists to find. Non-zero is the catch. It is not a root-cause tool.

Drop and pause metrics are per-mille, not percent. A value of 1 means 0.1%, which is already Broadcom's warning threshold for out-of-order packets; 10 is critical. Do not read them as packet counts.

RX missed errors means the NIC ring buffer had no free descriptor and the packet was dropped before the driver saw it — distinct from RX dropped (discarded higher up) and RX errors (arrived damaged). It is the clearest signal that a NIC is being overrun.

Pause / PFC separate a congested fabric from a failing NIC: the host asking the switch to slow down is a different problem from the host losing frames.

Blind spot: there are no disk media-error counters anywhere in the vSAN Performance Service, and no NIC ring-buffer utilisation — only overflow after the fact. A green screen here does not mean the hardware is healthy.
